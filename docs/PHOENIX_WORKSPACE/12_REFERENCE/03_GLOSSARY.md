# Phoenix — Glossary

Version: 1.0

Status: Approved

Owner: Phoenix Project

Last Updated: July 2026

---

# 1. Purpose

This document defines the official terminology used throughout the Phoenix project.

Its objectives are:

- Establish a common vocabulary.
- Eliminate ambiguity.
- Improve documentation consistency.
- Support onboarding.
- Facilitate communication across technical, business, and operational domains.

Every defined term has a single authoritative meaning.

---

# 2. Glossary Principles

The glossary follows these principles:

Single definition

Consistent terminology

Technology-neutral where possible

Business and technical alignment

Continuous evolution

Definitions should remain stable over time.

---

# 3. Architecture Terms

## Architecture

The overall structure of the Phoenix platform, including components, interactions, and design principles.

---

## Component

An independent software element with a clearly defined responsibility.

---

## Module

A logical grouping of related functionality.

---

## Service

A software capability exposed through a defined interface.

---

## API

Application Programming Interface used for communication between software components.

---

## Interface

A contract describing how components interact.

---

## Dependency

A relationship where one component relies on another.

---

# 4. Provider Terms

## Provider

A software component capable of retrieving information from an external source.

---

## Provider Registry

The centralized catalog of all registered providers.

---

## Provider SDK

The framework used to build Phoenix providers.

---

## Parser

Component responsible for extracting structured information from external content.

---

## Mapper

Component converting parsed information into Phoenix standard objects.

---

## Validator

Component verifying data quality before acceptance.

---

## Capability

The category of searches a provider supports.

Examples:

Vehicles

Watches

Electronics

General Marketplace

---

# 5. Search Terms

## Search

A user request processed by Phoenix.

---

## Query

The normalized representation of a search request.

---

## Finder

The search engine coordinating provider execution.

---

## Search Result

A standardized result returned by Phoenix.

---

## Ranking

The ordering process applied to search results.

---

## Aggregation

The combination of results coming from multiple providers.

---

# 6. Business Terms

## Customer

An individual or organization using Phoenix.

---

## Subscriber

A paying customer.

---

## Subscription

Recurring commercial agreement providing access to premium features.

---

## Conversion

Transformation of a free user into a paying subscriber.

---

## KPI

Key Performance Indicator used to measure business performance.

---

## Revenue

Income generated by the platform.

---

## Churn

Percentage of subscribers who discontinue their subscription.

---

# 7. Operations Terms

## Deployment

Technical installation of software into an environment.

---

## Release

Business availability of a software version.

---

## Monitoring

Continuous observation of system health.

---

## Incident

An event negatively affecting production services.

---

## Rollback

Restoration of a previous stable platform version.

---

## Backup

Protected copy of critical business assets.

---

## Recovery

Restoration of services after failure.

---

# 8. Governance Terms

## ADR

Architecture Decision Record documenting an important project decision.

---

## Knowledge Base

The complete collection of Phoenix documentation.

---

## Master Record

The master operational summary of project evolution.

---

## Traceability

The ability to follow relationships between decisions, documentation, and implementation.

---

## Governance

Processes ensuring documentation quality, consistency, and evolution.

---

# 9. Security Terms

## Authentication

Verification of user identity.

---

## Authorization

Verification of permissions after authentication.

---

## Least Privilege

Security principle granting only required permissions.

---

## Zero Trust

Security model assuming no implicit trust.

---

## Secret

Sensitive information such as credentials or encryption keys.
