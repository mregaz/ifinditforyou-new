# Phoenix — Decision Matrix

Version: 1.0

Status: Approved

Owner: Phoenix Project

Last Updated: July 2026

---

# 1. Purpose

This document defines the decision governance model for the Phoenix platform.

Its objectives are:

- Clarify decision ownership
- Standardize approval processes
- Improve traceability
- Reduce ambiguity
- Support long-term governance

Every significant decision should have a clearly defined owner and approval path.

---

# 2. Governance Principles

Phoenix decision-making follows these principles:

Clear ownership

Appropriate authority

Documented rationale

Traceable outcomes

Risk-based governance

Decisions should be made at the lowest appropriate level while maintaining architectural integrity.

---

# 3. Decision Categories

Phoenix classifies decisions into six categories.

Technical

Architecture

Operations

Business

Security

Strategic

Each category follows a dedicated approval process.

---

# 4. Decision Levels

## Level 1 — Operational

Low impact.

Examples:

Bug fixes

Minor refactoring

Documentation updates

Configuration changes

Approval

Responsible developer.

---

## Level 2 — Technical

Medium impact.

Examples:

New component

New API

Performance optimization

Provider implementation

Approval

Technical owner.

---

## Level 3 — Architectural

High impact.

Examples:

Architecture evolution

Framework changes

Infrastructure redesign

New platform capability

Approval

Architecture review.

ADR required.

---

## Level 4 — Business

Business impact.

Examples:

Pricing

Subscription model

Revenue model

Strategic partnerships

Market expansion

Approval

Business owner.

Documentation update required.

---

## Level 5 — Security

Security impact.

Examples:

Authentication

Authorization

Secrets management

Compliance

Infrastructure security

Approval

Security review.

ADR recommended.

---

## Level 6 — Strategic

Platform-wide impact.

Examples:

Vision evolution

Technology migration

International expansion

Product positioning

Business transformation

Approval

Project governance.

ADR mandatory.

---

# 5. Decision Ownership

Every decision must identify:

Decision owner

Decision date

Decision category

Impact level

Approval status

Related ADR

Affected documentation

Clear ownership improves accountability.

---

# 6. Decision Inputs

Good decisions require:

Business context

Technical analysis

Operational impact

Security implications

Risk assessment

Alternative evaluation

Decisions should be evidence-based whenever possible.

---

# 7. Decision Outputs

Every approved decision produces:

Implementation

Documentation updates

Architecture updates

Operational changes

Knowledge Base updates

Monitoring adjustments (if required)

A decision is complete only after implementation and documentation.

---

# 8. Decision Review

Review decisions when:

Architecture evolves

Business strategy changes

Technology becomes obsolete

Operational incidents occur

New regulations appear

Review ensures long-term relevance.

---

# 9. Escalation Rules

Escalate decisions when they:

Affect multiple systems

Introduce significant risk

Require cross-domain coordination

Impact customers

Modify strategic direction

Increase operational complexity

Escalation protects platform stability.

---

# 10. Decision Traceability

Every major decision should be traceable to:

ADR

Architecture

Business Strategy

Operations

Sprint documentation

Implementation

Master Record

Traceability supports governance and auditing.

---

# 11. Decision Quality Criteria

A high-quality decision is:

Documented

Justified

Reviewable

Consistent

Implementable

Measurable

Aligned with project objectives

Decision quality directly influences platform quality.

---

# 12. Continuous Improvement

The decision framework evolves through:

Operational experience

Architecture reviews

Business evolution

Lessons learned

Retrospectives

Governance reviews

Decision-making improves over time.

---

# 13. Strategic Conclusion

Effective governance is built on disciplined decision-making.

The Decision Matrix ensures that every significant choice within Phoenix is made with appropriate authority, documented rationale, and complete traceability, supporting sustainable platform evolution.
