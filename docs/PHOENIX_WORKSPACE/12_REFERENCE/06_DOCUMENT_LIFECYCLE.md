# Phoenix — Document Lifecycle

Version: 1.0

Status: Approved

Owner: Phoenix Project

Last Updated: July 2026

---

# 1. Purpose

This document defines the lifecycle of every document within the Phoenix Knowledge Base.

Its objectives are:

- Standardize document creation.
- Ensure documentation quality.
- Maintain consistency.
- Support governance.
- Preserve project knowledge.
- Prevent obsolete documentation.

Every document follows a defined lifecycle.

---

# 2. Lifecycle Principles

Phoenix documentation follows these principles:

Documentation First

Single Source of Truth

Continuous Improvement

Version Control

Traceability

Controlled Evolution

Documentation evolves together with the platform.

---

# 3. Lifecycle Stages

Every document passes through the following stages.

Draft

↓

Review

↓

Approved

↓

Published

↓

Maintained

↓

Archived

↓

Retired

Each stage has specific responsibilities.

---

# 4. Draft

Purpose

Initial creation of a document.

Characteristics

Incomplete

Subject to change

Internal only

Activities

Initial writing

Research

Cross references

Initial review

No operational dependency.

---

# 5. Review

Purpose

Validate quality before publication.

Review includes:

Accuracy

Architecture consistency

Business alignment

Operations impact

Terminology

Formatting

Governance compliance

Review improves quality before approval.

---

# 6. Approved

Purpose

Official acceptance.

Approval confirms:

Correctness

Completeness

Consistency

Ownership

Version assignment

Strategic alignment

Approved documents become official project knowledge.

---

# 7. Published

Purpose

Make documentation available for operational use.

Published documents:

Become official references

Can be cited

Can support implementation

May be referenced by ADRs

May influence future work

Publication marks operational availability.

---

# 8. Maintained

Purpose

Keep documentation aligned with project evolution.

Maintenance activities include:

Version updates

Architecture changes

Business evolution

Operational improvements

Provider additions

Security updates

Maintenance preserves documentation quality.

---

# 9. Archived

Purpose

Preserve historical knowledge.

Archived documents:

Remain accessible

Are read-only

Contain historical information

May support audits

May support future decisions

Historical knowledge remains valuable.

---

# 10. Retired

Purpose

Remove obsolete documentation.

Retirement requires:

Replacement identified

Dependencies reviewed

Cross references updated

Archive completed

Navigation updated

Retired documents should never disappear without trace.

---

# 11. Version Evolution

Documentation versions evolve according to:

Major

Structural changes.

Minor

Functional additions.

Patch

Editorial corrections.

Version history should remain available.

---

# 12. Ownership

Every document has an owner responsible for:

Accuracy

Updates

Review

Version management

Governance compliance

Knowledge preservation

Ownership guarantees accountability.

---

# 13. Lifecycle Review

Review documentation lifecycle:

After major releases

After architecture evolution

After governance updates

After organizational changes

At annual documentation review

Lifecycle governance evolves continuously.

---

# 14. Continuous Improvement

Documentation should continuously improve:

Clarity

Completeness

Consistency

Accessibility

Maintainability

Traceability

Quality increases over time.

---

# 15. Strategic Conclusion

A managed document lifecycle ensures that Phoenix documentation remains reliable, current, and valuable throughout the entire evolution of the platform.

Knowledge is preserved not only by writing documents, but by governing their complete lifecycle.
